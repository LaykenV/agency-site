# Facebook Group Outreach

**Channel:** Founder-led outreach from local Facebook business-promotion threads

**Cadence:** Every Monday morning, plus fast manual follow-up when someone replies

**Primary goal:** Close the first unrelated, market-rate Acadiana Web Design client

**Offer:** $0 down, from $199/month, 12-month minimum; build, hosting, domain, edits, and support included

This is separate from the paid Meta Messenger campaign in `CONTENT-PLAN.md`. Paid
campaign results and organic group outreach must be tracked as different channels.

## The source post

The first working source is the recurring promotion thread in **Acadiana Business
Hub**, posted by group admin Clay Young. The August 3 thread said:

> Please post, promote, market your Local Acadiana Small Business!!

The observed thread was:

`https://www.facebook.com/groups/acadianabusinessdirectory/permalink/2029994030952312/`

Business owners reply with their business names, descriptions, logos, services,
and sometimes contact information. That makes the thread useful because:

- The businesses are local and currently active.
- The owner has voluntarily identified the business and is trying to attract
  customers.
- The comment and linked Facebook Page provide enough public context to qualify
  the business before reaching out.
- Missing, unfinished, or weak websites are visible without manufacturing a
  problem that the owner may not have.

This is not permission to spam every commenter. The advantage comes from reading
the thread, selecting businesses that genuinely fit, and sending a relevant human
message while the post is still current.

## Monday operating routine

Run this every Monday morning when the new promotion thread appears.

1. Find the current week's business-promotion post.
2. Read every new business comment before messaging anyone.
3. Open the linked Facebook Page and check the website, services, recent activity,
   reviews or recommendations, location, and available work photos.
4. Exclude poor-fit businesses and anyone already contacted through another
   group or channel.
5. Select the best 5–10 prospects and record why each one fits.
6. Send a short, individually written Messenger message that references the
   business and the actual website gap.
7. Respond quickly when someone answers. Human response speed matters more than
   sending a larger batch.
8. Build a preview only after the owner explicitly accepts the offer or states
   that they actively need a website.
9. Send one no-pressure follow-up after 2–3 days if a qualified prospect does not
   reply. Stop after that.
10. Update the weekly scorecard before the next Monday.

Do not automate bulk Facebook messages. Keep every message reviewable and comply
with each group's rules.

## Who is worth messaging

The best-fit prospect is a local owner-operated service business with evidence
that customers already trust it but no strong website to convert that attention.

### Strong signals

- No website is listed, or the link is only a Facebook profile.
- The website says “coming soon,” is broken, is visibly outdated, or is difficult
  to use on a phone.
- The business serves Acadiana and sells a clear local service.
- The Page has recent activity, real work photos, and recommendations or reviews.
- The owner is actively promoting the business in the weekly thread.
- The service is commonly won through calls, texts, estimates, or quote requests.
- The Page contains enough real material to make a truthful concept.

Highest-fit categories include cleaning, landscaping, tree service, towing,
pressure washing, HVAC, plumbing, electrical, painting, roofing, fencing, pest
control, general contracting, and similar local services.

### Skip or deprioritize

- A mature, professional website is already in place.
- The business already works with a web agency.
- The offer is not a local service or does not fit the managed-site model.
- The Page appears inactive, unverifiable, or has broadly poor customer feedback.
- There are no usable assets and the owner has not agreed to provide any.
- The same owner has already received an outreach message elsewhere.
- The group rules prohibit unsolicited direct messages or commercial follow-up.

### Quick qualification score

Score each category from 0–2. Message businesses scoring 7 or higher first.

| Signal            | 0                           | 1                    | 2                                        |
| ----------------- | --------------------------- | -------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Website gap       | Strong current site         | Weak or unclear site | No, broken, or coming-soon site          |
| Service fit       | Poor fit                    | Possible fit         | Core local-service fit                   |
| Business proof    | Little evidence             | Some activity        | Reviews plus recent real work            |
| Contact value     | Low-value or unclear action | General contact need | Calls, texts, quotes, or bookings matter |
| Preview readiness | No usable material          | Logo or photos       | Logo, services, and multiple work photos |

## Outreach messages

Messages should sound like one local business owner speaking to another. Do not
paste the exact same paragraph to everyone.

### No website found

> Hey [first name]! I saw [business] in the Acadiana Business Hub promotion post.
> You've already built some good proof with [specific recommendation, work, or
> > service], but I couldn't find a website where people can see your services and
> request a quote. I build fully managed websites for Acadiana service businesses.
> Would you like me to send you a quick homepage idea? No pressure.

### Website is coming soon or unclear

> Hey [first name], I saw [business] in the Acadiana Business Hub post. I noticed
> the website is still on a coming-soon page. I build managed websites for
> Acadiana service businesses. Would it be useful if I sent you a quick homepage
> idea for the business? I wasn't sure whether you already had someone working on
> it.

### They accept the concept

> Awesome. Send me your logo, a few good photos, and the main services you want
> featured. I'll put together a concept so you can see the direction.

If the public Facebook Page already contains suitable assets, ask permission or
tell the owner that the concept will use those Page assets before publishing the
preview on the AWD domain.

### They actively need a website

> Absolutely. I'm thinking a clean, professional site that explains what you do,
> shows your work, and makes it easy for people to call or request a quote. If you
> send me your logo, a few good photos, and the main services you want featured, I
> can put together a free concept today. If you like the direction, my managed
> website plan is $0 down and starts at $199/month with a 12-month commitment.
> That includes the build, hosting, updates, and support.

### Send the completed preview

> Awesome! I went ahead and put together a full website concept using your logo,
> services, and a few photos from your Facebook Page:
>
> [preview URL]
>
> Take a look when you have a minute and let me know what you think. If you like
> the direction, I can customize the wording and photos with you and get it live.

### Polite not-now response

React politely and stop. Do not try to overcome an objection the person did not
raise. They can remain in a future-follow-up list only if they gave a concrete
timeframe or specifically invited another message.

## Pricing and expectation rules

- A concept preview is free; the production website is not free.
- Never say the prospect can “keep the site” unless an agreement explicitly
  grants that ownership.
- The standard public offer is $0 down, **from** $199/month, with a 12-month
  minimum.
- Disclose pricing immediately when someone says they actively need a website or
  asks what working together costs.
- For a prospect who only agreed to see an idea, send the preview first. Explain
  the managed plan after they react positively.
- Do not promise a same-day production launch before scope, assets, domain, and
  approval are confirmed. “Concept today” and “live website” are different
  commitments.

## Preview-page strategy

The preview is not a disposable screenshot or a generic hero. It is a polished,
unlisted landing-page concept that helps the owner imagine the business with a
real online home.

### When to build

Build only when at least one of these is true:

- The prospect explicitly says yes to receiving a concept.
- The prospect says they are currently looking for or need a website.
- A live conversation establishes real interest and the preview is the agreed
  next step.

Do not build previews for silent cold prospects, polite not-now replies, or every
commenter in a weekly thread. Cap speculative preview work at three qualified
prospects per week until this channel produces a market-rate close.

### Minimum source material

- Business name and verified service area
- Logo or current Page profile image
- Main services copied from or confirmed by the business
- One strong cover/hero image
- Three to six real work or product photos where available
- Verified phone, email, or preferred contact method
- Real recommendation/review proof only when it is visibly supported

Use public Facebook material only for the agreed unlisted concept. Confirm or
replace every asset and business claim before a production launch. Do not invent
testimonials, metrics, certifications, service areas, pricing, or guarantees.

### Reusable implementation

Lead previews use one shared system rather than a new route implementation for
every prospect:

- Public route: `/preview/[slug]`
- Prospect data registry: `lib/lead-demos.ts` (a discriminated union — each entry
  picks a `template`)
- Shared entry point: `components/lead-demo/LeadDemoPage.tsx`, which dispatches on
  `template`
- Prospect assets: `public/demos/[slug]/`

Two templates exist. Pick the one that matches how the business actually earns
trust; do not force a prospect into the wrong one.

| Template       | Fits                                                                                                          | Renderer / styles                                  | Look                                                                                                          | Example                            |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| `soft-service` | Consumer services sold on care and reviews — cleaning, care, hospitality                                      | `SoftServiceDemoPage.tsx` · `lead-demo.module.css` | Blush paper, Cormorant Garamond italic, review-percentage hero                                                | `/preview/shays-cleaning-services` |
| `trade-field`  | Licensed trades whose proof is the finished work — marine construction, fencing, concrete, roofing, site work | `TradeDemoPage.tsx` · `trade-demo.module.css`      | Bone paper, deep green ink, Oswald condensed caps, full-bleed photo bands, spec sheet, sticky mobile call bar | `/preview/gator-constructors`      |

Theme colours live in `tokens.css` — `:root` carries the `soft-service` palette and
`.lead-theme-trade` scopes the `trade-field` one. Add a third template as another
scoped class rather than editing `:root`.

Each preview must:

- State clearly that it is an unlisted website concept.
- Use `noindex`, `nofollow`, and `noarchive` metadata.
- **Show a real visual asset in the hero, above the fold.** Never ship a
  text-only hero. The owner decides in the first two seconds whether the page
  looks like _their_ business; type alone does not do that. Use the strongest
  available asset — a work photo or cover image first, the logo as the minimum
  fallback. If the only usable asset is a white-background logo JPEG, place it
  on the tinted paper with `mix-blend-mode: multiply` rather than shipping a
  hard white box.
- Use the prospect's real phone, text, or email CTA.
- Avoid live forms, analytics, tracking pixels, or third-party integrations before
  the prospect agrees to proceed.
- Work at 320, 375, 414, 768, and desktop widths without horizontal overflow.
- Use truthful alt text and accessible link names.
- Be built and checked locally, deployed, and then verified on the production URL
  before the link is sent.

The preview URL is unlisted, not password-protected. Anyone with the link can open
it even though search engines are instructed not to index it.

### Preview build checklist

1. Confirm the prospect accepted the concept.
2. Capture the Page facts and inspect the actual images.
3. Pick the template that fits the business (see the table above).
4. Add an entry to `lib/lead-demos.ts`.
5. Add optimized assets under `public/demos/[slug]/`. Never deploy a preview whose
   images are still placeholders — check the folder's `README.md` for the exact
   filenames each prospect needs.
6. Render the full page through the shared preview component.
7. Verify calls, texts, email links, copy, recommendation counts, and disclosure.
8. Run typecheck, diff checks, and the production build.
9. Inspect desktop and required mobile widths. Look at every image with your own
   eyes, including the hero asset — do not infer from the text layer that images
   rendered. A wrapper that computes `display: inline` silently collapses
   `aspect-ratio` and paints `fill` images at zero width, so the page reads as
   complete while showing nothing. Confirm each image has non-zero measured size.
10. Commit only the prospect preview and its required assets.
11. Deploy and verify the live route, robots metadata, and asset responses.
12. Send the preview manually and record the response.

## Finding additional Facebook sources

Do not immediately join dozens of groups or repeat the same message across them.
Add one source at a time and prove that it produces qualified conversations.

Look for recurring posts containing phrases such as:

- “Promote your business”
- “Drop your business below”
- “Small Business Monday”
- “Support local businesses”
- “What do you do?”
- “Business networking thread”
- “Share your Page”

Candidate places to inspect include town/community groups, Acadiana networking
groups, parish business groups, local chamber or referral groups, and groups
already visible in the signed-in Facebook account. Before using any source:

1. Read the group rules.
2. Confirm the promotion thread is recurring and current.
3. Confirm business owners, not consumers, actually participate.
4. Check whether direct outreach is allowed or culturally accepted.
5. Avoid contacting the same business from multiple groups.
6. Track results by group so productive sources can be distinguished from noisy
   ones.

Possible groups already visible in the account include Ask Lafayette,
Youngsville Business Networking, What Up Acadiana!, and What's Going On
Youngsville. These are candidates to evaluate, not approved outreach sources;
their current rules and recurring threads still need to be verified.

Update this table every Monday after outreach and again when replies, calls, or
closes occur.

| Week       | Source group/thread   | Comments screened | Qualified | Messages sent | Replies | Positive replies | Previews | Calls | Closes | New MRR | Notes                                                              |
| ---------- | --------------------- | ----------------: | --------: | ------------: | ------: | ---------------: | -------: | ----: | -----: | ------: | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 2026-08-03 | Acadiana Business Hub |                 — |         3 |             3 |       3 |                2 |        1 |     0 |      0 |      $0 | First test; Shay preview live, Charlie active need, Gerald not now |

Definitions:

- **Qualified:** Fits the local managed-site offer and has a real website gap.
- **Reply:** Any human response, including not-now replies.
- **Positive reply:** Accepts a concept, states an active need, or agrees to talk.
- **Preview:** A production-verified unlisted concept sent to the prospect.
- **Call:** A real sales/discovery conversation, not a Messenger exchange.
- **Close:** Signed agreement and successful first subscription payment.

## Decision rules

- Run this every Monday for at least four weeks before making a channel verdict.
- Keep messages selective. A high reply rate from 5–10 relevant messages is more
  useful than a low-quality blast to every commenter.
- If replies occur but previews do not lead to calls, improve the preview handoff
  and pricing conversation rather than adding more groups.
- If calls occur but nobody closes, inspect qualification, offer clarity, and
  objection handling.
- If a group produces no qualified businesses for three consecutive threads,
  deprioritize it.
- Add a second recurring group only after the Monday routine is being completed
  reliably and deduplication is working.
- Scale the channel after the first market-rate close by repeating the source,
  message type, prospect profile, and follow-up path that produced it.
